# Search and verification

## Define the boundary

- **Named area.** Record which towns, jurisdictions, or other boundaries the request covers. Avoid substituting a distance for a named boundary.
- **Straight-line radius.** Record the public origin, coordinates or precise reference point, radius, and units. Measure to the public business location with a reliable map radius tool or great-circle calculation. Compare unrounded distances to the boundary and then round for display. Check candidates close to the edge when location precision could change their eligibility. Keep unresolved edge cases separate from verified results.
- **Travel constraint.** Use a routing source and record mode, origin, date, and relevant time-of-day assumptions. A time limit and a road-distance limit are distinct. Keep optional commute estimates separate from straight-line distance. State any routing gaps.

If the user specifies a city but no starting point for a radius, use its named public central point and disclose it when a reasonable interpretation exists. Ask when several places or interpretations could materially change the results. Use the units the reader supplied or the normal local convention and show the units everywhere.

## Find and check candidates

For a supplied-only directory, label the verification as a review of supplied records and state the date those records describe. Apply the same geographic and status rules to that snapshot and identify details that need independent checking.

1. Search the requested area and nearby neighborhoods within it using relevant business types, professional terms, and local sources. Map listings and directories can supply candidates. Document the main search channels, coverage, and date.
2. Verify trading name, operating status as far as public evidence allows, location, and a public contact through first-party sites, current professional profiles, or appropriate official registers. Use dated secondary evidence when primary material is missing and label the gap. Public channels can include a contact page, business phone, or public professional inbox.
3. Check evidence separately for career fit and current availability. A portfolio, documented past mentoring, or a general contact invitation can support a lead. A dated active posting or current explicit invitation can support a confirmed opening. Record the source, date checked, and any expiration. An archived posting and unsigned guesses from map results do not establish availability.
4. Deduplicate by organization and premises. Keep branches with distinct eligible locations or opportunities as distinct entries. Avoid padding with outside-area or weakly related listings. If results are scarce, summarize the coverage and suggest nearby areas or alternatives as a separately labeled optional next search.

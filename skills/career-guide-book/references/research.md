# Research and evidence

- Use the authority for the applicable jurisdiction for licensing, permits, supervised practice, credential recognition, and mandated training. Distinguish legal requirements, typical employer preferences, and workbook recommendations. Add a check with the appropriate authority when requirements vary across localities.
- Use authoritative labor data for earnings and job outlook. Give geography, period, and population or occupation when relevant. Show the basis for cost or time estimates and distinguish them from requirements. Use timely first-party sources for course prices, application deadlines, and entry requirements.
- Map career claims that affect decisions to short source IDs or linked notes on the relevant page. Record each source's publishing organization, title, URL, date checked, and what it supports in the source register. Include visible short references for paper readers. An optional digital source companion can carry additional URLs, while the book should retain the citations needed to use it independently.
- When the user supplies sources, record which were usable and which were unavailable when that would affect the result. Describe meaningful conflicts and favor the applicable authority for rules. If the reader requests no browsing or research access is unavailable, state the evidence used and flag unresolved jurisdiction-specific and time-sensitive claims. Give focused verification steps. For a purely illustrative book, a short note explaining the evidence scope is sufficient.
- Keep demographic examples and sample career materials fictional or user-provided. Use the minimum necessary personal detail and avoid putting private addresses, undisclosed contacts, account information, or workspace identifiers into the output.
